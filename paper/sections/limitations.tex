\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}
\subsection{Implications for Local Decision Inference}
The measurements support inference specialization as a practical route to local
structured decisions. Small models provide a CPU-only deployment option;
consumer GPUs improve execution rate; and selective CPU expert placement extends
the model capacity available to a memory-constrained GPU. The resulting design
space permits applications to select model quality and resource requirements
without changing the decision interface. Full GPU placement yields the strongest
recorded request rate for the larger model, while offloading enables execution
on the lower-capacity A770 at increased cost.

These findings establish feasibility for the tested workloads and hardware.
They do not imply uniform performance across consumer devices, constant latency
as candidate sets grow, or accurate decisions from every supported model.
The present implementation also serializes recurrent continuations, leaving
independent-request concurrency as a potential optimization subject to state
isolation and numerical validation.

\subsection{Comparison with Dedicated Decision Models}
Jev is a relevant comparison target for functionality, decision quality, and
execution efficiency. Establishing parity requires matched states, candidate
meanings, answer types, and evaluation rules, together with a pinned Jev version.
The Qwen3.6-35B-A3B and Jev 1.13 accuracy results both cover the full MMLU
test set. Prompting, scoring, and aggregation remain potential sources of
difference. A matched
performance comparison should distinguish local
inference costs from remote service latency and report warm p50/p95 latency,
fixed-concurrency throughput, startup overhead, and failure rates. A same-model
constrained-generation baseline is additionally needed to separate inference
method from model capability.

Probability calibration is a further distinction: predictive confidence need
not agree with empirical correctness~\cite{calibration}. JET normalizes likelihoods
over the candidate set; these values are not calibrated correctness estimates.
NLL provides a probabilistic diagnostic but does not replace calibration curves,
Brier scores, or an evaluation of selective prediction. Claims about uncertainty
quality consequently require evidence beyond output-type validity and top-1
accuracy.

\subsection{Threats to Validity}
The principal Qwen3.6-35B-A3B and Jev accuracy results cover full MMLU.
Execution ablations and smaller-model comparisons use fixed subsets, and timing
repetitions are limited.
Quantization, placement, hardware, and execution versions differ between studies;
results from separate tables are not interchangeable. The prefix comparison
bundles reuse and cache-management changes, so it does not isolate parallel
probing. Candidate wording, token length, and answer priors can influence
likelihood ranking, and pretrained-model training data may overlap benchmark
content.

Sampler-free selection eliminates sampling variance but not numerical variation
across kernels, devices, or batch layouts. Exact agreement is therefore reported
only for tested configurations. The multimodal interface has been implemented,
but quantitative visual accuracy and closed-loop control evaluation remain
outstanding. Finally, raw experiment artifacts are retained outside version
control; independent replication requires those artifacts or regeneration under
the documented configurations.
